\begin{frame}[t]
  \frametitle{The Cosmic Microwave Background}
  \framesubtitle{What is it?}

  \begin{minipage}[t]{0.8\textwidth}
    \vspace{0pt}
    \begin{itemize}
      \item<1-> ~13.8 billion years ago the universe was much smaller.
      \item<2-> The entire universe was as dense and hot as a star.
      \item<3-> Plasma: Filled with unbound protons, neutrons, electrons and photons.
      \item<4-> As the universe expanded, it cooled and eventually released these photons at the {\em epoch of recombination}.
      \item<6-> ``Let there be light'' (X rays).
      \item<7-> These X-rays have now been stretched to microwaves.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{minipage}%

\section{What does the CMB tell us}
\begin{frame}[label=tell_us]
  \frametitle{What does the CMB tell us?}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item<1-> The CMB is a crystal clear picture of the universe when it was only 300,000 years old (epoch of recombination).
    \item<2-> It therefore provides a unique insight into the birth of the universe.
    \item<3-> It gives crucial data to suggest and rule out theories of the beginning of time.
    \item<4-> More than this, modern astrophysics uses the CMB as a ``back light'' for the dark regions of the universe.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
